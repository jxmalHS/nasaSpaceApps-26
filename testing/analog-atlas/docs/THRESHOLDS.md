**Strict** = breaking it marks the site "Fails a limit". **Soft** = score × 0.75 and a warning. Limits are kept strict; every NASA site that breaks one is listed as a tagged exception:

- `EXCEPTION · field site`: breaks it at the field site itself (NASA site-scale data)
- `EXCEPTION · 55 km cell only`: the 55 km cell breaks it, the field site passes
- `EXCEPTION · cell, unverified`: the cell breaks it; no finer NASA data to check (rain)

## 4. Sources

- **nasa-eo-ndvi**: NASA Earth Observatory, "Measuring Vegetation (NDVI & EVI)", 30 Aug 2000. https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/measuring-vegetation-ndvi-evi  
  "Very low values of NDVI (0.1 and below) correspond to barren areas of rock, sand, or snow. Moderate values represent shrub and grassland (0.2 to 0.3), while high values indicate temperate and tropical rainforests (0.6 to 0.8)." Caveat on the same page: "The numbers on the figure above are representative of actual values, but real vegetation is much more varied."
- **nasa-biome-desert**: NASA Science, "Mission: Biomes - Desert" (page updated 5 Dec 2025). https://science.nasa.gov/kids/earth/mission-biomes/biodesert  
  "Deserts get about 250 millimeters (10 inches) of rain per year—the least amount of rain of all of the biomes." The same page also says: "Most deserts receive less than 300 mm a year compared to rainforests, which receive over 2,000 mm."
- **mcd12q1-igbp**: Sulla-Menashe & Friedl, MODIS Collection 6.1 Land Cover Type (MCD12Q1) Product User Guide, Table 3 (IGBP legend). NASA LP DAAC. https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1409/MCD12_User_Guide_V61.pdf  
  Table 3 (IGBP legend): "Permanent Snow and Ice ... At least 60% of area is covered by snow and ice for at least 10 months of the year." "Savannas ... Tree cover 10-30% (canopy >2m)." "Woody Savannas ... Tree cover 30-60% (canopy >2m)." "Barren ... At least 60% of area is non-vegetated barren (sand, rock, soil) areas with less than 10% vegetation."
- **mod10-snow**: Hall & Riggs (2021), MODIS/Terra Snow Cover Daily L3 Global 500m SIN Grid, Version 61 (MOD10A1), NASA NSIDC DAAC, doi:10.5067/MODIS/MOD10A1.061; Riggs, Hall & Román (2019), MODIS Snow Products Collection 6.1 User Guide v1.0. https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MOD10A1.061  
  C6.1 user guide: "snow cover always has an NDSI > 0 but not all features with NDSI > 0 are snow." NSIDC MOD10A1 page: "The biggest limitation to the use of the MODIS snow-cover products is cloud cover"; "Polar darkness prevents snow-cover mapping in polar regions during the winter"; "Areas of ephemeral snow cover and very thin snow cover may not be mapped by MODIS".
- **nasa-field-seasons**: NASA SVS 14583, "Artemis & JETT5 Interview with Kelsey Young" (May 2024); USGS Astrogeology, "Artemis III mission tests San Francisco Volcanic Field" (11 Jul 2024); NASA D-RATS 2022 Test Report (NTRS 20230017073, XM-23-003), p. 6. https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14583  
  JETT5: "NASA conducted a week-long moonwalk simulation in the San Francisco Volcanic Field" ("May 13 to 17, 2024", USGS). D-RATS 2022 (October 2022, Black Point Lava Flow): "plan and execute 2 weeks of consecutive field-testing with little to no breaks between crews".
- **nasa-power-merra2**: Reichle & Liu (2014), "Observation-Corrected Precipitation Estimates in GEOS-5", NASA/TM-2014-104606 Vol. 35, sections 3.1.4 and 4; NASA POWER methodology. https://gmao.gsfc.nasa.gov/pubs/docs/Reichle734.pdf  
  "MERRA-2 is forced with its own, model-generated precipitation at high latitudes, with the corrected precipitation data over low latitudes, and with a combination of the two between 42.5º and 62.5º latitude." NASA POWER: "Meteorological parameters continue to be based upon MERRA-2 and GEOS FP-IT assimilation models."
- **merra2-dust**: NASA GMAO MERRA-2 aerosol diagnostics M2TMNXAER v5.12.4 (DUSMASS), NASA GES DISC. https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/ges-disc-m2tmnxaer-5.12.4  
  Source of the dust layer. The dataset documentation gives no suitability threshold.

Precedent check: Each limit is checked against the sites NASA actually used for that test. Vegetation and snow are re-measured at the field coordinates (~5 km box, 2023) from NASA GIBS: MOD13A3 v6.1 NDVI (doi:10.5067/MODIS/MOD13A3.061, annual maximum) and MOD10CM v6.1 snow cover (doi:10.5067/MODIS/MOD10CM.061, months with mean cover >= 60%, the IGBP permanent-snow line). Rain uses the 0.5 deg cell (NASA POWER / MERRA-2): no finer NASA rain product is available without a login. Sites whose index cell is over 60 km away are not counted.
